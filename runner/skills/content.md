# Content rules (restructuring / rewriting)

- Preserve meaning. A restructure may reorder, split, merge, tighten, or
  expand — it may not change what the document claims. Facts, numbers, names,
  dates, and links survive verbatim unless the comment explicitly asks
  otherwise.
- Keep the heading hierarchy coherent: one idea per heading level, no skipped
  levels, and a heading's text must still describe what sits under it after
  the rewrite.
- Keep the author's voice. Tighten sentences without flattening style; do not
  swap the document's register (formal/informal) unless asked.
- When shortening, cut redundancy first — repeated points, filler phrases,
  throat-clearing openers — before cutting substance. When the comment asks
  for expansion, add only material grounded in what the document already says.
- Preserve inline markup through the rewrite: links keep their `href` and
  stay attached to the same referent; emphasis (`strong`/`em`) and `code`
  spans move with the words they mark.
- Lists: keep parallel grammatical structure across items. Convert prose to a
  list (or back) only when the comment asks for it or the content is plainly
  enumerable.
- Do not delete content the reviewer did not ask you to remove; if something
  seems obsolete, keep it and flag it in the decision note.
- A restructure may span blocks: edit every block the ask covers (one edit per
  block) and add new blocks via inserts where the structure needs them. What
  edits and inserts cannot express, describe in the decision note.
